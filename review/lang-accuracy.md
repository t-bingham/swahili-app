# Swahili Curriculum — Language Accuracy Audit

**Auditor:** Swahili linguistics review (Claude)
**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Source audited:** `public/swahili_default.db` (the seed curriculum). No sibling `SwahiliApp/data/` directory exists; the SQLite DB is the source of truth. Grammar notes live in the `units.grammar_notes` column.

---

## Summary

The **hand-curated vocabulary, phrases, example sentences, and noun-class labels are excellent** — natural glosses, correct orthography, culturally grounded in East Africa, and idiomatic. However, the **machine-generated grammar content has two large systematic errors** that produce thousands of incorrect or non-existent word forms:

1. **Negative verb conjugations are wrong across the board** (negative present, negative past, negative perfect) — ~3,600 cards.
2. **Adjective–noun concord is wrong for N-class, Ji-Ma, and U-class nouns**, generating non-words like `nbaya` and `nkubwa` — ~1,000+ cards.

Because these are generated by templates, each bug repeats identically over ~159 verbs and dozens of nouns. Fixing the generator rules (not the individual cards) will correct them all at once. These should be fixed before the conjugation/grammar tracks are considered teaching-ready, as they currently teach learners incorrect Swahili.

---

## Method & Coverage

- Wrote a temporary Node script using the bundled `sql.js` to dump `cards` and `units` to JSON (script removed after use).
- **25,353 cards total**: 18,180 conjugation (generated), 4,508 grammar (generated), 2,665 vocabulary (generated label but hand-curated content).
- **Vocabulary:** reviewed ~120 cards in full across units 01–05, 22, 27 (greetings, numbers, colours, family, food, animals, body), plus spot checks elsewhere.
- **Conjugation:** fully traced the verb `soma` (all ~80 forms), and the monosyllabic verbs `la` (eat), `ja` (come). Verified bug scope with COUNT queries across all 159 verb roots.
- **Grammar/concord:** reviewed all adjective-agreement and noun-class cards in units 07, 10, 15.
- **Example sentences & grammar notes:** sampled ~10 each.
- Verb-root spelling, noun-class tags, and one subjunctive form were cross-checked; `aje` (not `akuje`) confirmed via reference (see Sources).

Sampling was deliberate: I traced generator *templates* exhaustively rather than spot-checking random cards, because generated content fails uniformly — one correct/incorrect template = N correct/incorrect cards.

---

## Strengths

- **Vocabulary glosses are natural and accurate.** e.g. `kali` = "sharp / spicy / fierce / strict", `pole` = "sorry / take it easy / condolences", `mzee` = "elder / old person", `ndugu` = "sibling / relative / comrade". Multi-sense glosses are well chosen.
- **Noun-class tags are correct** on the words sampled: `chakula` Ki-Vi, `mkate` M-Mi, `jina`/`maji`/`tunda` Ji-Ma, `rafiki`/`saa`/`siku` N-N, `mtu`/`mama`/`mke` M-Wa.
- **Cultural register is right:** `shikamoo`/`marahaba` (elder greeting + response), `ugali` glossed as "stiff maize porridge", `wali` vs `uji`, `bibi` = grandmother/madam. East-Africa-appropriate throughout.
- **Example sentences are idiomatic:** "Habari yako?", "Mambo vipi?", "Sawa sawa." — exactly how the words are used.
- **Affirmative conjugations are correct,** including the hard cases: monosyllabic verbs retain `-ku-` in present/past/future/perfect (`anakula`, `alikula`, `atakula`, `amekula`) and drop it in the subjunctive (`akule`). Conditional (`ningesoma`), conditional-past (`ningalisoma`), subjunctive (`nisome`), and object infixes (`ninakusoma`, `anamsoma`) are all right.
- Grammar-note tables (markdown) for tenses are clear and correct.

## Issues

### CRITICAL / CERTAIN — Negative conjugations (systematic, ~3,636 cards)

The negative-tense templates apply the wrong morphology. Traced on `soma`, identical pattern for all 159 verbs.

**1. Negative present — final vowel not changed (~1,212 cards).**
Generated: `sisoma, husoma, hasoma, hatusoma, hamsoma, hawasoma`.
Correct: Bantu verbs change final **-a → -i** in the negative present:
`sisomi, husomi, hasomi, hatusomi, hamsomi, hawasomi`.
Bonus collision: generated `husoma` (2nd-sg negative) is identical to the habitual `husoma`, which is impossible in correct Swahili (the -i ending disambiguates).

**2. Negative past — uses -li- instead of -ku- (~1,212 cards).**
Generated: `silisoma, hulisoma, halisoma, hatulisoma, hamlisoma, hawalisoma`.
Correct: negative past marker is **-ku-**, not -li-:
`sikusoma, hukusoma, hakusoma, hatukusoma, hamkusoma, hawakusoma`.

**3. Negative perfect — uses -me- instead of -ja- (~1,212 cards).**
Generated: `simesoma, humesoma, hamesoma, hatumesoma, hammesoma, hawamesoma`.
Correct: negative perfect ("not yet") marker is **-ja-**:
`sijasoma, hujasoma, hajasoma, hatujasoma, hamjasoma, hawajasoma`.

**Fix:** correct the three template rules in the conjugation generator and regenerate. Single-point fix, ~3,600 cards corrected.

---

### CRITICAL / CERTAIN — Adjective concord for N-class / Ji-Ma / U-class (systematic, ~1,000+ cards)

The adjective-agreement generator does not apply Swahili concord rules; it appears to prepend a literal class letter. Found in units 07, 10, 15.

**1. N-class produces non-words `nbaya`, `nkubwa` (~1,027 cards match `n…`).**
Generated: `furaha nbaya`, `furaha nkubwa`, `daktari nbaya`, `daktari nkubwa`, etc.
Correct: N-class concord assimilates: n+b → **mb**, and drops before k:
- `-baya` → **mbaya** (e.g. `furaha mbaya`)
- `-kubwa` → **kubwa** (e.g. `furaha kubwa`)
- `-zuri` → **nzuri** (already correct).
`nbaya` and `nkubwa` are not possible Swahili words.

**2. Animate N-class people take M-Wa concord, not N-concord.**
Generated: `daktari nzuri / nbaya / nkubwa`.
Correct: animate nouns (people) take human concord regardless of class:
**daktari mzuri, daktari mbaya, daktari mkubwa** (pl. `madaktari wazuri`).

**3. Ji-Ma (class 5) over-applies the `ji-` prefix.**
Generated: `gari jizuri`, `gari jibaya`, `gari jikubwa`.
Correct: class-5 concord is Ø for these stems:
**gari zuri, gari baya, gari kubwa**. (The `ji-` augment only attaches to a small set of monosyllabic adjective stems, not `-zuri/-baya/-kubwa`.)

**4. U-class (11/14 abstract nouns) uses `u-` concord; should be m-/n- (Likely).**
Generated: `upole uzuri`, `upole ubaya`, `upole ukubwa`.
Correct: abstract U-nouns take adjective concord like class 3 (**m-**):
**upole mzuri, upole mbaya, upole mkubwa** (cf. `uhuru mkubwa` = great freedom). Confidence Likely — recommend native-speaker confirmation for edge stems.

**Fix:** implement proper concord (nasal assimilation + animacy override + class-5 zero prefix + U-class mapping) in the adjective generator and regenerate.

---

### MODERATE — Monosyllabic-verb negatives & object forms (CERTAIN)

Beyond the general negative bug above, monosyllabic verbs compound the error (traced on `la` "eat"):
- Negative present generated `hakula` → should be **hali** (drop ku-, -a→-i).
- Negative past generated `halikula` → should be **hakula** (ha+ku+stem). (Ironically the correct neg-past surface form was generated for neg-present.)
- Negative perfect generated `hamekula` → should be **hajala**.
- **Subjunctive of `ja`:** generated `akuje` → should be **aje** (ku- drops in subjunctive; confirmed via reference).
- **Object infix + monosyllabic stem:** generated `ananikula` ("he eats me") retains ku- → should be **ananila** (the object infix supports the stem, so ku- is dropped). Confidence Likely.

---

### MINOR

- **`ndio` = "yes"** (Possible). The more standard affirmative is **`ndiyo`**; `ndio` is widely used colloquially but many references treat `ndiyo` as the citation form. Consider listing `ndiyo` (with `ndio` as a variant). Low severity.
- **Habitual gloss is misleading** (Possible). All persons correctly map to invariant `husoma`, but each is glossed "I [habitual]", "you [habitual]", etc. Since `hu-` does not mark person, six identical cards differing only by an English person label may confuse learners. Consider a single card glossed "(habitually) reads — same for all persons".
- **Duplicate cards.** Several words appear multiple times (`kiti` ×4, `kitabu` ×4, `habari`/`jambo`/`mambo` ×2) — the same word seeded into multiple units. Not an accuracy error, but inflates the deck and may surface the same item repeatedly. Worth de-duping or making cross-unit references.
- **`gari` plural concord** not separately verified but follows from the Ji-Ma fix above (class 6: `magari mazuri`, etc.).

---

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the conjugation generator's three negative-tense rules** (neg present -a→-i; neg past -ku-; neg perfect -ja-), including monosyllabic stem handling, then regenerate. Highest priority — currently teaching incorrect forms to ~3,600 cards.
2. **Rewrite the adjective-concord generator** to implement: N-class nasal assimilation, animacy override (people → M-Wa), class-5 zero prefix, and U-class → m- concord. Regenerate (~1,000+ cards).
3. **Fix `ja` subjunctive** (`aje`) and object-infix monosyllabic forms (`ananila`).
4. Resolve `ndio`/`ndiyo`, the habitual person glosses, and duplicate cards (low priority).
5. **Have a native speaker spot-check** the regenerated negatives and concord, plus the U-class adjective forms flagged "Likely".

The vocabulary core is strong and needs no remediation. The generated grammar is the entire risk surface — and because it is template-driven, the whole problem is fixable at a handful of code sites.
